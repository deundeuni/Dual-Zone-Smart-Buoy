### Abstract

**[English Abstract]**  
This idea white paper proposes the concept of a Dual-Zone Smart Buoy, expanding the role of marine buoys from mere navigational aids (survival coordinates) to ecological habitats (ecosystem coordinates). Divided by the waterline, the Top Zone utilizes a mushroom microstructure surface and adheres to IALA standards to prevent barnacle fouling for navigation safety. Conversely, the Bottom Zone incorporates artificial reef surface treatments to intentionally encourage the settlement of barnacles, shellfish, and seaweed, forming a 5-tier vertical ecosystem. This paper aims not to claim engineering perfection or legal exclusivity, but to offer a direction for future sustainable marine infrastructure that coexists with ocean ecosystems.

### 1. Problem Statement: Limitations of Styrofoam Buoys and Single-Purpose Coordinates

* **Environmental Limitations and Regulatory Trends:** Most existing marine buoys are manufactured from styrofoam or simple polymer materials, breaking down into microplastics under wave action and sunlight. Consequently, domestic and international environmental regulations are tightening. For instance, the Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries (MOF) of South Korea is actively enforcing a complete ban on installing new styrofoam buoys in aquaculture farms and pushing for a 100% transition to eco-friendly buoys. Furthermore, toxic anti-fouling paints used to prevent biofouling are emerging as targets of global ocean pollution regulations.
* **Human-Centered Single Purpose:** Traditional buoys function solely as 'survival coordinates' for human navigation safety, sea markers, and territorial indications, acting as mere marine waste or obstacles to ocean life.
* **Core Question:** Can artificial buoy structures perform their navigational safety marker functions for humans while simultaneously functioning as 'ecosystem coordinates' providing safe habitats for marine life?

### 2. Core Concept: Waterline-Based Dual-Zone Structure & Biomimetic Spectrum

The waterline where the buoy rests serves as a functional boundary to completely bifurcate physical surface properties and operational objectives.

`[See Figure 1: DUAL-ZONE SMART BUOY CROSS-SECTION]`

* **Top Zone (Above Waterline: Anti-fouling Oriented)**
  * **Surface Property:** Smooth Mushroom Microstructure Surface application oriented. Mushroom-shaped micro-pillars reduce physical adhesion even when barnacle larvae (Barnacle Archetype) secrete bio-cement substances.
  * **Identification & Compliance:** Strives to adhere to IALA buoy coloring and marking standards, equipped with red/yellow visual markings, LED night beacons, and Radar Reflectors.
  * **Purpose:** Maintaining a clean and lightweight surface to serve as survival coordinates providing clear positional reference to navigators.

* **Bottom Zone (Below Waterline: Artificial Reef & Dynamic Ballast Oriented / Independent Auxiliary Entity)**
  * **Surface Property:** Surface treatment oriented toward creating micro-roughness and pores to facilitate the settlement of barnacles, shellfish larvae, and seaweed spores.
  * **Independent Auxiliary Entity Identity:** The Bottom Zone is treated not as a mere attached structure subordinate to the buoy hull, but as an independent ecosystem auxiliary module that can be connected, detached, and replaced via magnetic/mechanical quick-release clamping and mooring chains. This secures modular flexibility during harvesting, cleaning, or module replacement.
  * **Ecological & Structural Function:** Intentionally inviting biofouling to transform the lower buoy body into a small artificial reef, while utilizing the bio-mass weight as a natural self-ballasting mechanism to lower the overall center of gravity.
  * **Purpose:** Serving as ecosystem coordinates for marine life (Seaweed Archetype) while securing dynamic wave stability.

* **Modular Biomimetic 4-Matrix Spectrum**
  Defines surface control combination matrices adaptable to sea zone objectives and environmental conditions.
  * **Matrix 1 (Top Clean / Bottom Clean):** Full anti-fouling type (High-speed vessel lanes, military AtoN)
  * **Matrix 2 (Top Reef / Bottom Reef):** Full eco-induction type (Artificial reef dedicated, marine forest restoration zones)
  * **Matrix 3 (Top Clean / Bottom Reef):** Forward hybrid type (*Main concept of this white paper*: Navigational buoys, aquaculture infrastructure)
  * **Matrix 4 (Top Reef / Bottom Clean):** Inverted hybrid type (*Inverted structure*: Top facilitates seaweed settlement for surface nutrient removal and photosynthesis filtration; Bottom maintains a biofouling-free surface for underwater precision sensor & sonar protection)

* **Internal and Mooring Modules**
  * **Internal Assembly:** Sensor module (water temperature, salinity, water quality metrics), battery, and small-scale energy harvesting modules.
  * **Mooring Section:** Mooring chain connected to seabed anchors.

### 3. Function and Ecosystem Architecture: 5-Tier Vertical Ecosystem

The Bottom Zone of the buoy is envisioned to function as a 5-tier vertical habitat apartment in sea areas experiencing desertification.

* **Tier 1 (Base Substrate Tier):** Sessile organisms settle to form a solid biological foundation (e.g., Barnacles / Barnacle Archetype — substrate base).
* **Tier 2 (3D Outer Wall Tier):** Shellfish and oyster colonies grow to form porous 3D structures contributing to water filtration (e.g., Oysters, Mussels — filtration feeding and shelter provision).
* **Tier 3 (Dynamic Inhabitant Tier):** Small benthic organisms inhabit the created pores (e.g., Crabs, Shrimp — benthic crustacean food web formation).
* **Tier 4 (Vegetation Environment Tier):** Seaweed spores (Seaweed Archetype) settle on mooring chains and lower surfaces to perform photosynthesis and oxygen supply (e.g., Undaria, Sea lettuce — oxygen supply and habitat creation).
* **Tier 5 (Nursery Tier):** Juvenile fish sanctuary formed around seaweed forests and biological clusters avoiding predators (e.g., Juvenile Rockfish, Sea Bream — predator avoidance and nursery space).

### 4. Scalability and Extension: Smart Aquaculture and Fish Release Refuge

This concept strives to extend beyond basic buoys into integration with marine industries and resource enhancement projects.

* **Smart Aquaculture Infrastructure Integration**
  * Application Field — Smart Aquaculture Industry
  * Buoy Section — Bottom Zone lower section & Mooring Chain
  * Expected Effects — Utilizing mooring chains for seaweed seedling lines, encouraging natural oyster/mussel spat settlement, collecting real-time water temperature, salinity, and dissolved oxygen data via internal sensors to establish smart farm data infrastructure.

* **Fish Release Refuge Integration**
  * Application Field — Marine Resource Enhancement & Fish Stock Release Programs
  * Buoy Section — Bottom Zone lower 3D habitat space
  * Expected Effects — Mitigating high initial mortality rates associated with direct open-water releases, providing predator refuge and food web resources for 2–3 weeks post-release to enhance survival rates, establishing data tracking for released sea areas via sensors.

### 5. Future Value and Implementation Challenges: Recording Future Feasibility

* **Technical Challenges and Analytical Concepts**
  * Need for cost-effective mass production processes for top-side mushroom microstructures.
  * Need for regulatory and technical compliance review regarding existing navigational aid standards such as IALA.
  * **Inverted Buoyancy & Weight Balance Design Orientation:** Reinterpreting lower biofouling not as a mere weight burden, but as a 'natural self-ballasting and hydrodynamic damping' mechanism. Lower center-of-gravity displacement via attached shellfish enhances metacenter restoring moment ($GM$) against wind loads on the top zone, while seaweed drag cushions dynamic wave oscillations. However, this reinterpretation remains a conceptual proposal prior to empirical validation, and to prevent submersion due to excessive biofouling, early buoyancy design guidelines strive to limit `Expected Maximum Bottom Attached Weight = Under 30% of total reserve buoyancy`.

* **Future Value as an Idea White Paper**
  * Rather than immediate commercial product manufacturing, this white paper holds value in recording the possibility of a paradigm shift fusing eco-buoys, artificial reefs, aquaculture data infrastructure, and marine ecosystem restoration.
  * It is expected to serve as a conceptual seed when future researchers explore eco-friendly marine materials or integrated marine monitoring systems.

### 6. Prior Art Comparison and Self-Verification Testing Standards

* **Preliminary Reference Notice:** Prior studies, patent information, and verification standards listed in this section are preliminary citations referenced during concept formulation. This does not represent a complete prior art search conducted by formal patent analysis, and this white paper claims no legal exclusivity.

* **Natural Precedent:** The attachment of barnacles to the skin of cetaceans (Cetacea) represents a biological case of commensalism, an observed natural example where a large host tolerates a minor burden without regarding small sessile organisms as a fatal threat. The Bottom Zone design of this white paper draws inspiration from this natural mechanism of tolerant coexistence.

  Recommended domestic and international voluntary reference standards for future empirical studies and inspection of this white paper concept include:

  * **Anti-Fouling & Bio-Settlement Evaluation Standards:** ASTM D3623 (Standard Test Method for Testing Antifouling Panels in Shallow Submersion), ISO 13073 / ISO 19073 (Ships and marine technology — Anti-fouling systems on ships)

* **Examples of Existing Prior Art**
  * **Anti-fouling Microstructures:** Microstructure-based biofouling prevention research from Kiel University (Germany), US Navy NOFOUL projects, etc.
  * **Reef-Combined Buoys:** Registered Patent `KR102050218B1` (Buoy combined with artificial reef functions), Published Patent `KR20130024655A` (Mooring buoy for seaweed attachment), and similar lower-reef structure patents.

* **Core Differential of This Idea**
  * Unlike existing technologies performing single functions ('anti-fouling' OR 'reef combination') or relying on simple physical assembly, this idea establishes the **waterline as an explicit functional boundary**, striving for top-side cleanliness as survival coordinates and intentionally inviting lower-side biofouling as ecosystem coordinates (independent auxiliary entity) within a unified **Dual-Zone integrated mechanism** and **4-Matrix spectrum**.

### 7. Closing Remarks: A Proposal for All Marine Structures

The Dual-Zone Smart Buoy proposed in this white paper is not engineeringly perfect. Deployed in real ocean environments, biofouling might progress faster than anticipated leading to buoyancy loss, or strong waves might erode the top microstructures.

However, the question and direction posed by this white paper remain clear.

> **"All artificial structures placed in the ocean should not remain selfish coordinates solely for humans, but must simultaneously serve as ecosystem coordinates for small marine lives in those waters."**
